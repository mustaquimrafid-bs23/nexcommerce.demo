# nexCommerce Brand Skill

Everything needed to produce on-brand nexCommerce content — positioning, voice,
visual identity, LinkedIn craft, and hard guardrails. Apply proactively; don't
wait to be asked for "brand guidelines."

> **Precedence.** This skill absorbs generic LinkedIn best-practice guidance
> (hooks, structure, length tiers, CTAs, file conventions). Where generic advice
> conflicts with anything in §5–§7 or §11, **this skill wins.** Section 7 lists
> the specific overrides so they don't get re-litigated every session.

---

## 1. What nexCommerce Is (positioning — internalize before writing)

**nexCommerce** is an AI-powered **agentic commerce** platform by
**Brain Station 23 / nopStation**, built for B2B, B2C, and marketplace businesses.

**The core idea:** it transforms every customer move into the *next best action* —
powering real-time, hyper-personalized, enterprise-scale commerce across vast
catalogs, millions of customers, and every business model, from one intelligent
platform.

**The philosophy (never break this):**
- The agent **understands, recommends, explains, assists.**
- The human **reviews, decides, approves, executes.**
- **Never portray AI as replacing people.** Always "human-led, agent-assisted."
- Concretely: write "flags the at-risk order before the slot is missed," not
  "reassigns it without waiting for a manager to notice." The second phrasing
  removes the human and reads as a governance risk to EU/UK enterprise buyers.

**Where it works:** the whole journey — starting at the **first search** (not
checkout), through discovery, personalized shopping, merchant intelligence,
operations, fulfillment, and delivery. Frame the problem as commerce being
*disconnected from the very first search onward* — never say it "breaks at
checkout" (that contradicts the product).

**Verified capability areas** (use these; confirm with the user before going
beyond them): intelligent/conversational search · personalized shopping · order
management · inventory (incl. batch-wise and multi-unit-of-measure) · hyperlocal
/ multi-node stock · fulfillment · delivery and geofenced routing · ERP sync ·
marketing and segmented promotions · analytics · mobile apps.

**Architecture facts that are safe to state:** microservices, API-first,
Kubernetes, Redis caching, RabbitMQ messaging. Treat deployment/ownership model
claims (source-available, tiered ownership) as commercial terms — confirm before
publishing.

**Agent count:** never state a fixed number of agents. Write "autonomous agents"
or name the specific capability. A number dates the copy and boxes in the roadmap.

## 2. Signature Language

**Brand term:** "agentic commerce" — NOT "agentic eCommerce." Prefer "commerce"
over "eCommerce" (broader, more premium, enterprise-appropriate).

**Tagline (sign-off):** *nexCommerce — Commerce that understands what happens next.*
Use as a closer, not mid-copy.

**Positioning phrases to reach for:**
- "turns/transforms every customer move into the next best action"
- "human-led, agent-assisted"
- "from the first search to final delivery"
- "real-time" · "hyper-personalized" · "massively scalable" · "enterprise-scale"
- "from one intelligent platform"

**Name styling:** lowercase-n **nexCommerce**. Never "NexCommerce" or
"Nexcommerce" — including in file names, slide titles, and image exports.

**Scale words:** "massively scalable" and "enterprise-scale" overlap — use ONE
per sentence.

## 3. Target Audience

**Primary:** e-commerce executives · retail leaders · digital transformation
teams · commerce technology decision-makers. Primary markets **UK and EU**,
then US.

**Secondary:** operations managers · product teams · business owners.

**Implication for tone:** this is an enterprise B2B audience evaluating whether
your AI claims are real. They reward substance and specificity, punish fluff and
overclaiming. Confident but not hypey. Never spec-sheet spam. If a claim can't be
backed by the product, cut it.

---

## 4. Brand Voice

- **Confident, intelligent, human, premium.** Reference points: Apple product
  film, Salesforce innovation film, modern SaaS launch.
- **Not:** sci-fi, hypey, robotic, corporate-stiff, or "excited to announce."
- **Not** LinkedIn-influencer register either: no "$200K invested. Zero revenue."
  confessional openers, no one-sentence-per-line staccato, no "Here's what I
  learned 👇" listicle voice. That style is common and effective on LinkedIn
  generally; it is wrong for this brand.
- Cool and declarative over explanatory. Short fragments for rhythm are good
  ("Every search. Every cart. Every delivery.").
- Trust the reader — say less, imply more.

## 5. Visual Identity

### Colours — measured from the official logo files (authoritative)

| Role | Hex | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Brand navy (primary)** | `#003371` | The wordmark colour. A true royal navy — do **not** substitute a near-black navy. |
| **Deep navy (bg base)** | `#012148` – `#0A1B3D` | Darker field for backgrounds only; the brand blue is `#003371`. |
| **Accent pink (tick, top)** | `#F13365` | Top of the checkmark gradient. |
| **Accent crimson (tick, tip)** | `#E60C45` | Bottom of the checkmark gradient. Use as the single hero accent. |
| **Cyan (secondary accent)** | `#3DE0FF` | Supporting highlight, eyebrow labels, hairlines. |
| **Electric blue** | `#4F8CFF` | Supporting only. |
| **Text on dark** | `#F8FAFF` white · `#D8DEE9` soft | |

The accent is a **pink→crimson gradient**, not a flat magenta. Earlier guidance
listing `#FF4DA6` / `#FF2E7A` as the brand pink was wrong — those are too purple.

**Crimson is semantically loaded.** Across the asset set it reads as
*problem / failure / before* (the failed search, the shortfall stat, the tangled
network). Do not use crimson to label a neutral or positive category — it creates
a false alarm signal. Reserve it for the failure half of a contrast, or for one
hero number.

**Background rhythm:** alternate navy ↔ deeper navy/indigo across a series so a
multi-slide carousel doesn't read as one flat field.

### Fonts

- **Headlines / hero statements:** **Outfit** (Bold/ExtraBold). Chosen because it
  shares the rounded geometric letterforms of the wordmark — headline and logo
  read as one type system.
- **Body, labels, eyebrows:** **Work Sans**.
- **Punchy social headlines only:** Big Shoulders Bold (condensed, all-caps) — use
  sparingly, never on the cover banner.
- Canva has all three; search the font name in the text panel.

### Logo

- Two official lockups exist: **navy** (`#003371`) and **white knockout**. Both
  include the "next generation e-commerce" subtitle.
- **Always use the official file.** Never recreate, retype, recolour, or
  pixel-edit it. There is no official subtitle-free lockup — cropping the
  subtitle is a derivation, not an approved asset.
- Give it clear space; never stretch.
- *Known tension to raise with the brand owner, not solve in Canva:* the subtitle
  says "next generation **e-commerce**," which contradicts the agentic-commerce
  positioning and the "commerce over eCommerce" rule.

### Visual feel

Premium navy base, cyan/crimson accent glows, real people (not robots), clean UI,
generous negative space. A fine particle-network texture is the established
background system.

**Avoid:** robots, holograms, circuit boards, sci-fi, stock-photo clichés,
fabricated dashboards with garbled text, world-map dot textures, and the
neon purple/magenta swirl that is the default "AI startup" LinkedIn look.

## 6. LinkedIn Post Craft

### Hook (first 1–2 lines — only these show before "…see more")

Lead with a sharp observation, a bold declarative, a concrete scene, or the
tagline. Never "We're excited to announce" or "Check out our new video."

Working hook patterns for this brand:
- **Contrarian/deflating:** "Most retailers have AI now. Most of them aren't getting anything from it."
- **Recognisable scene:** "There's a moment every online shopper knows quietly well."
- **Category challenge:** "Commerce starts breaking at the very first search."
- **Tagline lead:** "nexCommerce — Commerce that understands what happens next."

### Body

- **ONE idea per post.** Short paragraphs, 1–3 lines. Whitespace between thoughts.
- Weave in signature phrases naturally; never stack five adjectives into a spec list.
- Ground claims in something real — a metric, a named mechanism, a cited stat.
- Company-page posts use the platform as the acting subject, not "we."

### Length

| Tier | Words | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| Short | 120–200 | Single observation, stat post, event note |
| Standard | 200–350 | Default. Agent stories, industry POV |
| Long | 350–500 | Framework or analysis pieces only |

If a post exceeds ~500 words it should be a carousel or a LinkedIn article.

### Close

End on the tagline as a sign-off, or a genuine question that invites a real
answer ("Does your platform's order data feed anything, or does it just get
archived?"). For video: "See it in action 👇" — point at the content, don't
announce that you made it.

### Hashtags

- Very end only, after a line break. 3–5 (6 max).
- **Default set:** `#agenticcommerce #retailtech #ecommerce #AI`
  (add `#enterprise` `#CX` `#brainstation23` as needed).
- **Event set (eCommerce Expo):** `#ece26 #ecommerceexpo #agenticcommerce
  #retailtech #ukretail`.
- Keep `#nexCommerce` in the set for brand-search recall.

### Links

- Prefer the **first comment** for URLs on video/reach posts. If in body, place at
  the very bottom, after the tagline, before hashtags.
- Event posts where the link IS the CTA may keep it in body.
- Don't drop `sales@` addresses into brand-story posts — it undercuts the restraint.

### Publishing mechanics

- Upload video **natively**, never as a link.
- **Carousels: publish as a single merged multi-page PDF (document post).** Native
  multi-image posts do not produce a swipe/peek cue on mobile or desktop, and
  document posts earn stronger dwell-time signal.
- Reply to comments in the first hour. Tag @nexCommerce, @Brain Station 23,
  @eCommerce Expo inline via "@".

## 7. Formatting Overrides

These override generic LinkedIn advice. Do not reintroduce them.

| Generic advice | What to do instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Bold key phrases with `**asterisks**`" | Never. Write plain text. | LinkedIn renders no markdown — asterisks appear literally and look broken. |
| "Use ✓ and → bullet decorations" | Prose and line breaks. At most one arrow, for a swipe cue. | Decoration stacks read as templated content-marketing, not premium. |
| "Use 1–3 emojis" | Zero in graphics. At most one per post in copy, and only pointing at content. | The user's standing instruction. No emoticon faces in visuals, ever. |
| "Write with 'you', 'I', 'we'" | "You" is fine. Avoid **we/us/our** as the acting subject — the platform acts. | Standing instruction; also reads more confident. |
| "Ask clarifying questions first" | For nexCommerce, audience/tone/voice are already fixed here. Ask only about the *angle* and whether a visual is needed. | Re-asking settled questions wastes the user's time. |
| "ALL CAPS for emphasis" | Never in body copy. Caps only in display headline lockups. | |

## 9. Proof and Claims Hygiene

The audience is specifically evaluating whether the AI claims are real. Sloppy
evidence costs more than no evidence.

- **Cite every external statistic** on the graphic itself — source and year, small
  caps under the number. An uncited stat from an unfamiliar vendor reads as invented.
- **Hedge causality.** Write "alongside the search rollout, organic traffic rose
  70%," not "once search went live, traffic rose 70%." Single-cause claims invite
  the objection you can't answer.
- **Keep the numbers in.** "Increased engagement and conversion" is weaker than
  saying nothing. If a metric exists, put it on the asset.
- **Reference customers:** the flagship proof is a South Asian grocery chain and
  the brand name won't be recognised in UK/EU. Lead with the scale
  ("800+ outlets, 60k+ households a day"), which travels, rather than the name.

## 10. Canva / Graphic Workflow

1. **Canvas:** 1080×1080 (feed) or 1080×1350 (portrait — more feed space, use for
   carousels). Cover banner: 1128×191.
2. **Background:** navy base, soft radial glow in cyan (one corner) and crimson
   (opposite corner) at low opacity, plus the fine particle-network texture.
3. **Type:** Outfit Bold headline, Work Sans body/labels. One bold headline plus
   one supporting line per graphic.
4. **Accent:** ONE crimson or cyan element per graphic. Don't rainbow it.
5. **Badge:** the "AGENTIC COMMERCE PLATFORM" pill at the top is the series
   identity marker — keep it consistent across every asset.
6. **Logo:** official file, bottom-centre or bottom-left, clear space, subtitle intact.
7. **Restraint:** generous negative space. Premium = uncrowded. One idea per slide.
8. **Carousel:** alternate background depth across slides; each slide stands alone;
   last slide = tagline + CTA. Export as one merged PDF.

### Pre-ship check on every graphic

- No text overlapping a shape, bar, or another text block
- Content fills the canvas — no dead band above the bottom edge
- Nothing clipped at the frame edge (brackets, rules, glows)
- Every geometric element looks deliberate, not like a rendering artifact
- Legible at feed thumbnail size, not just at full size

## 11. Guardrails (do not violate)

- Don't say commerce "breaks at checkout" — it starts at first search.
- Don't portray AI acting *instead of* humans; always human-led, agent-assisted.
- Don't claim capabilities beyond §1 without user confirmation.
- Don't use "NexCommerce" casing anywhere, file names included.
- Don't state a fixed number of agents.
- Don't stack "massively scalable" + "enterprise-scale" in one breath.
- Don't recreate, retype, or recolour the logo — use the official file.
- Don't use crimson for a neutral or positive label.
- Don't use markdown asterisks, emoji faces, or ✓/→ decoration stacks.
- Don't use sci-fi / robot / hologram / world-map imagery.
- Don't publish an uncited statistic.

## 12. Output Convention

When drafting posts as files, save to:

```
LinkedIn-Posts/YYYY-MM-DD_topic-keywords.md
```

With this header, then the post copy exactly as it should be pasted:

```markdown
# nexCommerce LinkedIn Post: [Topic]

Date: YYYY-MM-DD
Format: single image | carousel PDF | text-only | video
Audience: [segment]
Visual: [asset filename, or "none"]

---

[post copy — plain text, paste-ready, no markdown formatting]

---

Hashtags: #...
```

## 13. Quality Checklist

Before shipping any post:

- Hook works as the first two lines alone, with the rest hidden
- One idea, not a feature list
- Human-led framing intact — no agent acting *instead of* a person
- No "we/us/our" as the acting subject
- Every claim traceable to §1, or cited
- Casing is `nexCommerce` everywhere
- No markdown asterisks, no emoji faces, no decoration stacks
- Closes on the tagline or a real question
- 3–5 hashtags, at the very end
- Paired visual passes the §10 pre-ship check
